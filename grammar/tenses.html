ENGLISH GRAMMAR • TENSES

English Tenses

Tenses tell us when an action happens — in the present, past or future. Learn the 12 tenses with simple structures, rules and examples.

What are Tenses?

A tense is a form of a verb that helps show the time of an action or state. English commonly describes time through three main periods: present, past and future.

Quick idea: Each time period has four common forms — Simple, Continuous, Perfect and Perfect Continuous. Together, these make the 12 commonly taught English tenses.

12 Tenses at a Glance

TenseBasic StructureExample
Present SimpleSubject + V1(s/es)She works every day.
Present ContinuousS + is/am/are + V-ingShe is working now.
Present PerfectS + has/have + V3She has finished the work.
Present Perfect ContinuousS + has/have been + V-ingShe has been working for two hours.
Past SimpleS + V2She worked yesterday.
Past ContinuousS + was/were + V-ingShe was working at 5 PM.
Past PerfectS + had + V3She had finished before I arrived.
Past Perfect ContinuousS + had been + V-ingShe had been working for two hours.
Future SimpleS + will + V1She will work tomorrow.
Future ContinuousS + will be + V-ingShe will be working at 5 PM.
Future PerfectS + will have + V3She will have finished by 5 PM.
Future Perfect ContinuousS + will have been + V-ingShe will have been working for two hours.

How to Learn Tenses

Don't try to memorize all 12 forms at once. First understand the difference between time and aspect. Then learn the structure and practice with real sentences.

1. Learn the time

Ask: Is the action about the present, past or future?

2. Learn the form

Ask: Is it simple, continuous, perfect or perfect continuous?

3. Look for time clues

every day → often Present Simple   |   now → often Present Continuous
yesterday → often Past Simple   |   by tomorrow → often Future Perfect
